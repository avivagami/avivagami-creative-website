First, I did some market research

What the market told us.

Prebiotic soda is growing fast, but it was built on vague gut-health promises. Four findings shaped every FORM decision, from the name to the end line.

$1.95BPepsiCo's price for Poppi, 2025

The category is real, and the giants are in.

US prebiotic and "better soda" sales are around $1.5B a year, nearly all of it Olipop and Poppi. Olipop doubled to about $400M in 2024 at a $1.85B valuation, and Coca-Cola launched Simply Pop in 2025.

So FORM

Can't win by shouting "gut health" louder than PepsiCo. It needs one idea the big brands don't own.

$8.9MPoppi's settlement over gut-health claims

Shoppers have stopped trusting the claim.

A class action argued Poppi's prebiotic fiber was too small a dose to matter. Fiber across the category ranges from about 2 g to 9 g a can, and the front of the can rarely says which.

So FORM

Puts the proof on the front: 6 g fiber, 3 g sugar, a 2:1 ratio anyone can check. Numbers before adjectives.

<10%of US women meet their fiber needs

There is a fiber gap, and a trend to match.

The average American eats about 15 g of fiber a day against a 25 to 38 g recommendation. "Fibermaxxing" became one of the most talked-about food trends of 2025.

So FORM

Leads with fiber, not probiotics: a three-fiber blend at 6 g, and a UGC storytime that rides the fibermaxxing trend.

49%of US adults trying to drink less in 2025

The buyer is a label-reader who still wants a treat.

Prebiotic sodas over-index with young women bored of diet soda. Gen Z already makes up a quarter of Olipop's adoption, and 65% of Gen Z plan to drink less alcohol.

So FORM

Targets women 28 to 38 at two moments, the 4pm swap and the alcohol-free dinner, with a calm, not clinical voice.

In a category built on vague gut claims, FORM shows the ratio.

Iterations worth showing

Continuity

The table kept changing

In Lemon Left Mint the set jumped from studio to wood and the produce looked fake.

Approved keyframes in one locked set before animating.

Physics

The can that leaked

The can popped into frame, jumped and leaked.

Explicit rules (sealed, rigid, never leaves the surface) plus a timed shot list.

Cost

Lemon too big

Wrong scale in the two-shot.

An edit that changed only the lemon: 250 credits, not a full regeneration.

Edit

Jump cut at 0:04

A push-in mismatch between two clips.

A crop punch-in and a three-frame dissolve. No regeneration.

Model choice

Veo vs Kling

The lockshift can-opening, run on both models.

Kling gave better hand motion at about a third of the cost.
